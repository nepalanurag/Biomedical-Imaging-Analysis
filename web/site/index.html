COVID lung infection on CT

Segmentation and quantification demo. Pick a slice, see the infected regions.

This page runs a simple, interpretable pipeline on chest CT slices from a COVID-positive patient: threshold the Hounsfield units to find the lungs, threshold again to find infected-looking tissue (ground glass in the -700 to -200 HU band), and report what fraction of the lung is affected. No neural network, no black box. The results below are precomputed from the full 3D scan; the method and its limits are described at the bottom of the page.

Demo only, not a medical device. The scans come from the public MIDRC-RICORD-1A research dataset.

Try your own image

Upload a lung-window CT slice (grayscale PNG or JPEG). The page shows it and gives a rough on-screen estimate by inverting the standard lung window back to approximate Hounsfield units. This is approximate: there is no lung mask in the browser, so treat it as a sketch, not a measurement. The four samples above carry the real segmented results.

How it works, and where it can be wrong

  1. Lung mask. Keep voxels between -950 and -300 HU, fill holes, keep the largest connected region. This is the standard cheap lung segmentation; it can leak into the bowel gas or miss collapsed lung.
  2. Infection mask. Keep voxels between -700 and -200 HU inside the lung mask, then median-filter to drop specks. This band covers ground-glass opacity, the hallmark COVID pattern on CT, but it also catches vessels, partial-volume edges, and motion blur. A threshold cannot tell those apart.
  3. Score. Infected pixels divided by lung pixels, per slice and for the whole scan. The original version of this project divided by the wrong denominator and reported values over 100%; that bug is fixed here and documented in the repo.

On the full scan (255 slices, one patient) the fixed method finds 9.69% of lung volume affected. Full analysis, figures, and code: github.com/nepalanurag/covid-ct-web.
